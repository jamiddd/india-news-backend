Privacy Policy

Open Indian News · Last updated: 2 October 2026

This policy explains what information Open Indian News ("the app", "we", "us") collects, why, and how you can have it deleted. We've written it to describe exactly what the app actually does — not a generic template.

Information we collect

When you sign in (with Google, or with an email and password) via Firebase Authentication, we store:

  • Your email address and display name, as provided by your sign-in method.
  • A stable account identifier (Firebase's user ID) and which sign-in method you used.
  • Your app preferences — theme, accent color, language filter, which news categories you've enabled or added, sources you have boosted, your chosen zodiac sign for the horoscope, and your notification choices — so they carry over if you sign in on another device.
  • Your profile photo, if you have one. If you sign in with Google, the photo address from your Google account is stored; if you upload your own, the image file is stored in our file storage (see Third parties) under a name that includes your account identifier.

We also store, once you are signed in:

  • What you follow — the stories, topics and news sources you follow, and the sources you have blocked — so we can notify you about them and shape your feed.
  • Daily-game progress — which daily games you have played on which dates, and how far you got.
  • A log of notifications we have sent you (which kind, and when), so we never send the same one twice or exceed the daily limits.
  • Which timelines you have opened (a one-time count per timeline), and a record of which stories were shown to you in the feed's “explore” slot, used to learn which suggestions are useful.
  • Stories you save, so they follow you to another device (see below).
  • Which stories you have opened — along with roughly how long you spent on each, how far down you scrolled, and whether you opened the media-framing panel or expanded a summary — to rank the "For You" feed. This never leaves our server and is not sold or shared.
  • A push notification token for each device you enable notifications on, so we can deliver them. It is issued by Firebase Cloud Messaging, identifies the app install rather than you or your hardware, and is deleted as soon as you turn every notification off again — or, along with everything else, when you delete your account.

We do not collect your location, contacts, advertising ID, hardware or phone identifiers, or your browsing history outside the app. (The app does create two random, anonymous identifiers that are not hardware identifiers: the push-notification token above, and the daily-poll identifier described under Daily poll.)

Notifications

Notifications are opt-in and each kind has its own switch in Settings → Notifications. Depending on what you turn on, the app can send: breaking news, a daily digest at times you choose, updates on stories and topics you follow, a Morning Brief (about 6:30 AM IST), a Late-Night Wrap-up (about 8 PM IST), and a daily horoscope (about 7 AM IST). Turning every switch off deletes your push token from our server.

Premium purchases

Premium (Monthly or All-Time) is bought through Google Play, which handles your payment and billing under Google's own privacy terms; we never see your card or payment details. Premium status is kept on your device and is tied to your Google account through Google Play, not to your Open Indian News account — Restore purchases in the app asks Google Play to confirm it again. When you buy or restore, the app sends the Google Play purchase token to our server, which checks it with Google to confirm it is genuine and currently paid for. We do not store the token or keep a record of your purchases, and it is not linked to your account.

Donations

The app is free to read. It accepts an optional donation, which unlocks nothing and is separate from Premium — see our Refund & Cancellation Policy.

We never see or store your card, UPI ID, or any other payment details. Payment is handled entirely by Razorpay on their own page; we only receive confirmation from them that a payment happened, its amount, and its transaction ID. If you were signed in when you donated, we store that transaction alongside your account ID so we can find it if you ask for a refund. Donating while signed out is possible, and then we hold no link to you at all.

Saved stories

Stories you save are kept on your device, and — if you are signed in — also synced to our server so they appear on any device you sign in to. If you are not signed in, they stay on your device only, and uninstalling the app or clearing its data loses them permanently.

Deleting your account (Settings → Delete Account) removes the server-side copy along with everything else.

Daily poll

The daily poll can be answered without an account. To stop one device voting twice, the app generates a random identifier for the installation, and our server stores your vote together with a one-way (HMAC) hash of that identifier — not the identifier itself. It is not linked to your account, email or name, so deleting your account does not remove it, and your vote stays in the poll's totals. Uninstalling the app or clearing its data creates a new, unrelated identifier.

Reports you send us

If you report a story, timeline or explainer from inside the app (signed in), we store the item you reported, the reason you chose, any note you wrote, and your account identifier, so the report can be reviewed. If you delete your account, the report is kept for moderation. The account identifier on it then points to an account that no longer exists, but anything you typed in the note remains, so please do not put personal details in it. You can ask us to delete a report you sent at any time.

Feedback you send us

If you use the feedback form on this website, we store what you typed: your message, the category you picked, and your name and email address if you chose to give them. Both are optional, and leaving them blank is a normal way to use the form — we would rather have an anonymous report than no report.

We do not store your IP address with your message, set a cookie, or attach any identifier to what you send. (Our server does see your IP address when you connect, as every server does, and uses it briefly to limit abuse, for example to cap how many messages one connection can send per hour.) An email address is used only to reply to you about that message, and for nothing else — not a mailing list, not an account. Feedback is kept while it is useful for fixing what it describes, and you can ask us to delete yours at any time.

Crash reporting

The app includes Firebase Crashlytics, so that when it crashes or freezes we find out and can fix it. A report is sent only when something actually goes wrong. It contains the technical details of the failure — where in the code it happened, your device model and Android version, and the state of the device at that moment, such as free memory and storage — together with a random identifier for the app installation.

That identifier is not your account, and we do not attach your name, email or user ID to a crash report. Crash reporting is on in the published app and off in development builds. It is the only automatic reporting sent from the app, and there is no way for us to use it to watch what you read. Separately, our servers record technical errors in their own operation (for example a failed request) through Sentry, an error-tracking service; this concerns how the server behaved, not your reading.

What we don't do

The app has no advertising SDK and no third-party analytics or attribution SDK — no Google Analytics, no Facebook SDK, no ad network, no data broker, nothing that follows you between apps or across the web. We do not sell or share your information with anyone for advertising or profiling, and there is nothing integrated that could. What we record about your reading is listed above, stays on our own server, and exists to order your feed.

Third parties

We use three services from Google, all part of Firebase, and each processes data under Google's own privacy terms:

  • Firebase Authentication handles sign-in. Google processes your email, display name and authentication credentials in order to provide it.
  • Firebase Cloud Messaging delivers push notifications, and issues the device token described above.
  • Firebase Crashlytics receives crash and freeze reports, as described above.

We also use Google Play to process Premium purchases (see Premium purchases above) and Razorpay to process donations, only if you make one (see Donations above).

Hosting. Our servers run on DigitalOcean, and our database and file storage (including profile photos, and images and audio used in the app) are hosted by Supabase. They store and serve data on our behalf; we do not give them any use of it beyond that.

Content and image services your device contacts. To show a story, a source logo or a video, your phone connects directly to services that necessarily see your IP address and device details, and the address being fetched:

  • Google's favicon service and logo.clearbit.com, for the logos of news sources shown in the feed.
  • YouTube (Google), when you play a video in the Watch or Shorts screens, which uses YouTube's embedded player and its thumbnails; YouTube's own privacy terms apply. Some videos play from the publisher's own video host instead.
  • Wikipedia / Wikimedia, for some images shown in the app.
  • The publishers' own image servers and CDNs, for story pictures.

Services that process content, not your data. Anthropic (Claude) generates story summaries, framing analysis, and the scripts for the Daily Brief, timelines and explainers, and Sarvam AI turns those scripts into spoken audio. They receive news content, not your personal information. Sentry receives technical error reports from our server (see Crash reporting). Brevo sends us internal alert emails.

Apart from the services above, we don't share your data with any third party.

Android backup

The Android app allows Android's automatic backup, which is on by default. If you have backup turned on in your phone's settings, Android may copy the app's local data — including your saved stories, preferences, Premium status on the device, the daily-poll identifier and your signed-in state — to your Google account, and restore it onto a new phone. That copy is held by Google under your Google account, not by us. You can turn it off in your phone's backup settings.

This website

The website loads its fonts from Google Fonts, so Google receives your IP address and browser details when you open a page. We set no cookies of our own; the only thing stored in your browser is your light/dark choice, on your device. If you donate on this site you are sent to Razorpay's page (see Donations).

News content

The app displays headlines, short snippets, images, and links to original articles from third-party news publishers. The app does not display full article text and we do not republish it — every story links back to the original publisher's site and is attributed to its source. Reading full articles happens on the publisher's own site, subject to their own privacy policy.

To group coverage, summarise it and compare framing, our servers fetch publicly accessible article pages and retain the text extracted from them, together with images and metadata such as the preview image. We do not access paywalled or login-restricted content. That article text is used internally and is sent to an AI provider (Anthropic's Claude) to generate summaries and framing; it is not shown to you as an article and it contains no information about you.

How long we keep your data

We keep your account information for as long as your account exists. If you delete your account (Settings → Delete Account, in the app), your account row and preferences are permanently removed from our database, and your Firebase Authentication identity is deleted — immediately, not after a delay.

Your rights

  • Access — you can see your current preferences at any time in the app's Settings screen.
  • Correction — update your display name/preferences directly in the app.
  • Deletion — delete your account and its associated server-side data at any time via Settings → Delete Account. This is immediate and permanent, apart from the few things listed as kept. See Delete your account for exactly what is removed, what is kept for tax purposes, and how to request deletion if you can no longer sign in.

If you're in India, these rights are consistent with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. If you have any question about your data that isn't covered by the in-app controls above, contact us at the address below.

Children's privacy

Open Indian News is a general-audience news app and is not directed at children. We don't knowingly collect information from children under 18. If you believe a child has created an account, contact us and we'll delete it.
